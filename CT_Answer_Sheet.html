Question 1 Solve the following system using the Gauss Elimination method:
2x + y − 3z = 11,   4x − 2y + 3z = 8,   −2x + 2y − z = −6
Answer

Write the augmented matrix $[A|b]$:

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -3 & 11 \\ 4 & -2 & 3 & 8 \\ -2 & 2 & -1 & -6 \end{array}\right]$$
Step 1 — Eliminate x from R₂ and R₃
$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -3 & 11 \\ 0 & -4 & 9 & -14 \\ -2 & 2 & -1 & -6 \end{array}\right]$$ $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + R_1$: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 2 & 1 & -3 & 11 \\ 0 & -4 & 9 & -14 \\ 0 & 3 & -4 & 5 \end{array}\right]$$
Step 2 — Eliminate y from R₃
$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + \dfrac{3}{4}R_2$:

New $R_3$: $\left[0,\ 0,\ -4+\dfrac{3}{4}(9)\ \bigg|\ 5+\dfrac{3}{4}(-14)\right] = \left[0,\ 0,\ \dfrac{11}{4}\ \bigg|\ -\dfrac{11}{2}\right]$
Step 3 — Back Substitution
From $R_3$: $\dfrac{11}{4}z = -\dfrac{11}{2}$  →  $\boxed{z = -2}$

From $R_2$: $-4y + 9(-2) = -14$  →  $-4y = 4$  →  $\boxed{y = -1}$

From $R_1$: $2x + (-1) - 3(-2) = 11$  →  $2x + 5 = 11$  →  $\boxed{x = 3}$
∴ x = 3,   y = −1,   z = −2
Verification: $2(3)+(-1)-3(-2)=11$ ✓,   $4(3)-2(-1)+3(-2)=8$ ✓,   $-2(3)+2(-1)-(-2)=-6$ ✓
Question 2 Solve the following system using the Gauss-Jordan method:
6x + 15y + 2z = 72,   x + y + 54z = 110,   27x + 6y − z = 85
Answer

For a convenient pivot, swap $R_1$ and $R_2$ first, then write the augmented matrix:

$$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 54 & 110 \\ 6 & 15 & 2 & 72 \\ 27 & 6 & -1 & 85 \end{array}\right]$$
Step 1 — Eliminate x from R₂ and R₃
$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 6R_1$,   $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 27R_1$: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 54 & 110 \\ 0 & 9 & -322 & -588 \\ 0 & -21 & -1459 & -2885 \end{array}\right]$$
Step 2 — Normalize R₂, then eliminate y from R₁ and R₃
$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 / 9$: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 54 & 110 \\ 0 & 1 & -35.7778 & -65.3333 \\ 0 & -21 & -1459 & -2885 \end{array}\right]$$ $R_1 \leftarrow R_1 - R_2$,   $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + 21R_2$: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & 89.7778 & 175.3333 \\ 0 & 1 & -35.7778 & -65.3333 \\ 0 & 0 & -2210.333 & -4257 \end{array}\right]$$
Step 3 — Normalize R₃, then eliminate z from R₁ and R₂
$R_3 \leftarrow R_3 / (-2210.333)$  →  $z$-row becomes $\left[0,\ 0,\ 1\ \big|\ 1.9260\right]$

Back-substituting into $R_1$ and $R_2$ gives the reduced identity matrix: $$\left[\begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & 0 & 2.4255 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 3.5730 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1.9260 \end{array}\right]$$
∴ x ≈ 2.4255,   y ≈ 3.5730,   z ≈ 1.9260
Verification: $6(2.4255)+15(3.5730)+2(1.9260) = 14.553+53.595+3.852 \approx 72.00$ ✓ (small rounding only)
Question 3 Find the real root of cos x = 3x − 1, correct to five decimal places, using the Fixed Point Iteration method.
Answer

Rearranging $\cos x = 3x - 1$ into the fixed-point form $x = g(x)$:

$$g(x) = \dfrac{\cos x + 1}{3}$$
Convergence Check
$g'(x) = -\dfrac{\sin x}{3}$,   so $|g'(x)| \le \dfrac{1}{3} < 1$ for all $x$.
The iteration scheme is guaranteed to converge.

Initial guess: $x_0 = 0.6$ (since the root clearly lies near $x=0.6$, where $\cos x \approx 0.825$ and $3x-1 \approx 0.8$)

Question 4 Find y(0.2) for y' = x − y², y(0) = 1, with step length h = 0.1, using the Modified Euler's method.
Answer

$f(x,y) = x - y^2$,   $x_0 = 0$,   $y_0 = 1$,   $h = 0.1$.

$$\textbf{Predictor: } \bar{y}_{n+1} = y_n + h\,f(x_n,y_n) \qquad \textbf{Corrector: } y_{n+1} = y_n + \dfrac{h}{2}\Big[f(x_n,y_n) + f(x_{n+1},\bar{y}_{n+1})\Big]$$
Step 1 — Find y(0.1)
$f(x_0,y_0) = 0 - 1^2 = -1$
Predictor: $\bar{y}_1 = 1 + 0.1(-1) = 0.9000$
$f(x_1,\bar{y}_1) = 0.1 - (0.9)^2 = 0.1 - 0.81 = -0.71$
Corrector: $y_1 = 1 + \dfrac{0.1}{2}\big[-1 + (-0.71)\big] = 1 - 0.0855 = 0.9145$
Step 2 — Find y(0.2)
$f(x_1,y_1) = 0.1 - (0.9145)^2 = 0.1 - 0.836310 = -0.736310$
Predictor: $\bar{y}_2 = 0.9145 + 0.1(-0.736310) = 0.840869$
$f(x_2,\bar{y}_2) = 0.2 - (0.840869)^2 = 0.2 - 0.707061 = -0.507061$
Corrector: $y_2 = 0.9145 + \dfrac{0.1}{2}\big[-0.736310 + (-0.507061)\big] = 0.9145 - 0.062169 = 0.852331$

Question 2 Solve the following system of equations using Cramer's Rule:
2x + y − z = 3,   x − y + 2z = 2,   3x + 2y − z = 5
Answer

Cramer's Rule: $x = D_x/D, \ y = D_y/D, \ z = D_z/D$, where $D$ is the determinant of the coefficient matrix.

Coefficient Determinant D
$$D = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = 2[1-4] - 1[-1-6] - 1[2+3] = -6+7-5 = -4$$
Replace Column 1 with b → D_x
$$D_x = \begin{vmatrix} 3 & 1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 2 \\ 5 & 2 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = 3[1-4]-1[-2-10]-1[4+5] = -9+12-9 = -6$$
Replace Column 2 with b → D_y
$$D_y = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 3 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 3 & 5 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = 2[-2-10]-3[-1-6]-1[5-6] = -24+21+1 = -2$$
Replace Column 3 with b → D_z
$$D_z = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 & 5 \end{vmatrix} = 2[-5-4]-1[5-6]+3[2+3] = -18+1+15 = -2$$
Compute x, y, z
$x = \dfrac{D_x}{D} = \dfrac{-6}{-4} = 1.5$    $y = \dfrac{D_y}{D} = \dfrac{-2}{-4} = 0.5$    $z = \dfrac{D_z}{D} = \dfrac{-2}{-4} = 0.5$
∴ x = 1.5,   y = 0.5,   z = 0.5
Verification: $2(1.5)+0.5-0.5=3$ ✓,   $1.5-0.5+2(0.5)=2$ ✓,   $3(1.5)+2(0.5)-0.5=5$ ✓
Question 3 Find y(0.2), correct to four decimal places, using the Taylor's series method, up to 3 terms, given dy/dx = x² + y, y(0) = 1.
Answer

The Taylor series expansion about $x_0 = 0$ is:

$$y(x) = y_0 + x\,y_0' + \dfrac{x^2}{2!}y_0'' + \dfrac{x^3}{3!}y_0''' + \cdots$$
Compute Successive Derivatives at x = 0
$y' = x^2 + y$  →  $y_0' = 0 + 1 = 1$
$y'' = 2x + y'$  →  $y_0'' = 0 + 1 = 1$
$y''' = 2 + y''$  →  $y_0''' = 2 + 1 = 3$
Substitute x = 0.2 (using 3 terms beyond y₀)
$y(0.2) = 1 + (0.2)(1) + \dfrac{(0.2)^2}{2}(1) + \dfrac{(0.2)^3}{6}(3)$

$= 1 + 0.2 + 0.02 + 0.004$
∴ y(0.2) ≈ 1.2240
Exact solution: $y = 3e^x - x^2 - 2x - 2$. At $x=0.2$: $y = 3(1.22140)-0.04-0.4-2 = 1.22421$. The 3-term Taylor estimate is accurate to within 0.0002 ✓
